# MTT Content Quality Pipeline

Adopted by the user on 2026-09-15. Applies to new and revised MTT SEO, GEO, Insights, Case Studies and customer-problem content without requiring another reminder.

## Workflow

Customer problem → search intent / available GSC signals → research → verified facts → packaging judgments → draft → remove generic AI language → factual and claim review → SEO → GEO → conversion → editorial score → build and deploy → observe available GSC data.

The objective is useful content for real B2B buyers and search systems, not article volume. Do not build a separate writing application for this workflow.

## 1. Define the problem before drafting

Record the target customer, actual problem, primary search intent and decision the reader needs to make. If there is no clear customer problem, do not write the article. Use GSC query–page evidence when available; do not invent search volumes or performance data when it is unavailable.

## 2. Research and classify claims

Verify current regulations, materials, market trends, technical standards and other changing information online. Prefer authoritative primary sources and retain supporting links.

Distinguish verified facts, documented MTT practical experience and project-dependent guidance. Only attribute experience to MTT when supported by genuine records. Otherwise frame engineering reasoning conditionally, such as “we would check,” rather than implying a completed project or test.

Never invent customers, orders, test results, savings percentages, factory size, production lines, certifications, damage rates or universal tolerances. Explain which project variables affect recommendations. Unsupported claims block publication regardless of score.

## 3. Add practical packaging value

Include at least 2–3 substantive packaging judgments: what to check first, failure modes, tradeoffs, structural decisions, sampling, production limitations, cost drivers, insert/support logic or shipping considerations. Explain reasons, rather than merely summarizing other websites.

Include at least three types of information gain: engineering judgment, buyer checklist, decision framework/table, comparison, scenario, failure example, tradeoff, production limitation, sampling advice, calculation or structure/material logic. Label illustrative scenarios appropriately; do not represent them as real customer cases.

A definition → benefits → types → why us → contact article without substantive additional value is not publishable.

## 4. Writing voice and final language review

Write as a packaging engineer and experienced B2B export salesperson: professional, clear, practical, specific, measured and natural. Explain uncertainty and tradeoffs. Delete sentences whose removal loses no information.

Avoid stock phrases in authored marketing copy:

- In today's competitive market; In today's fast-paced world; When it comes to
- Furthermore; Moreover; In conclusion; It is important to note; Whether you're…
- elevate your brand; seamless; game-changing; cutting-edge; revolutionary; best-in-class
- one-stop solution; leading manufacturer; best quality; competitive price; high quality at competitive prices

Avoid repetitive “It's not just X — it's Y,” artificial transitions, identical section lengths, mechanical three-bullet sections, excessive em dashes, keyword stuffing and empty conclusions. Use structure according to the problem, not a rigid article template.

Before publication, remove generic introductions, filler, repeated ideas, generic conclusions, exaggerated marketing, artificially balanced sentence patterns, unsupported claims and unnecessary length.

## 5. SEO and GEO

Define one primary search intent, a primary query, supporting queries, a unique title, H1, meta description, URL and relevant internal links. Natural language takes priority over keyword repetition. Preserve working URLs unless explicitly asked to change them.

Open articles with a direct Quick Answer of approximately 40–100 English words. Use independently useful answers where appropriate: what buyers should provide, what can go wrong, when an approach fits, what to test, and how to choose between options. Do not mechanically append FAQs.

Do not claim that a technical audit, originality score or content update guarantees Google indexing, ranking, traffic, AI citation or inquiries.

## 6. Positioning and commercial next step

MTT positioning: **China Packaging Development & Manufacturing Partner**.

Do not imply ownership of specific factories or production lines, unverified certifications, or third-party projects. Appropriate descriptions include packaging engineering, sampling, production coordination, manufacturing partners, QC coordination and China production support, consistent with verified capabilities.

Each article has one primary commercial next step tied to its problem. Ask for relevant product photos, dimensions, quantity, current structure, artwork or target market, and state what MTT can evaluate. Avoid a generic “Contact us today.”

## 7. Publication gate

Record an editorial assessment with evidence and remaining issues:

| Criterion | Maximum |
|---|---:|
| Customer problem clarity | 10 |
| Original insight | 20 |
| Technical usefulness | 20 |
| Human writing | 15 |
| SEO | 10 |
| GEO | 10 |
| Trust / claim safety | 10 |
| Conversion | 5 |
| Total | 100 |

85–100: eligible to publish after required checks. 75–84: revise first. Below 75: do not publish. Scores are editorial judgments, not objective search-performance measurements. An unresolved factual or misleading claim blocks publication even above 85.

Publish only when factual, useful, sufficiently original, natural and commercially relevant. Execute authorized changes through the repository's build, release checks and deployment workflow; do not merely hand back a deployment prompt when this agent can complete the work. Existing user authorization remains applicable.

After deployment, inspect available GSC evidence when the task includes follow-up. Record unavailable data honestly. Do not promise unattended monitoring unless it has actually been arranged.

## 8. Future machine checks

Vale, defluff and LanguageTool are candidates for a later automated review layer. This policy adoption does not install, configure or run them. Machine checks complement research and editorial judgment; they cannot establish factual accuracy or authentic MTT experience.
