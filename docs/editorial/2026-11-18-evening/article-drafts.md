# Draft 1 — How to Specify a Radial Insert for a Hinged Hexagonal Gift Box

**Candidate primary query:** hinged hexagonal gift box radial insert. **Status:** draft, unpublished; editorial scoring and link verification pending.

You have selected a six-sided presentation box and want customers to see a ring of individually presented pieces when they lift the cover. The next decision is the insert, not the exterior print: the layout must accommodate the largest actual wrapped item, leave a way to remove it and remain clear of the lid when the box closes. Treat the reference drawing as a starting shape; approve the final number and position of recesses against a physical sample.

## Start with the object, including its wrapping

Measure the maximum length, width and thickness of each packed item. Include the seam or twist of an individual wrapper, any label, and the way the item will enter its recess. For an assortment, compare every shape in the range rather than assuming that pieces with equal net weight have equal envelopes. Provide the supplier with a position map and an extra set of actual packed pieces for fitting.

Radial compartments converge toward the center. That creates a narrow tip even when the outside edge looks generous. Ask for a section and plan showing the usable area of each recess, the bridge between adjacent recesses and the room required for fingers to grip the item. A recess that fits an item when pushed straight down may make it hard to lift out again.

## Separate product retention from insert retention

The insert has to locate the items, while the box has to locate the insert. These are separate questions. Confirm whether the insert rests on the base, has a supporting layer beneath it or needs a finger notch to be removed. Ensure the insert does not ride into the hinge when the lid closes. Paperboard, wrapped board or other proposed supports should be reviewed with the actual item and finish; the reference image does not establish a material or thickness.

For a food gift, identify whether the pieces are individually wrapped and whether any packaging surface contacts food. Ask the appropriate supplier to document the material for the intended contact and destination; do not assume a decorative gift box or an unverified material is suitable for direct contact.

## Test the opening sequence

Place the full assortment in a representative sample, close the lid and reopen it in the way a customer would. Record whether the lid clears the product, the cover aligns with the base, and each item can be lifted out without disturbing its neighbours. If the closing system includes magnets, tabs or ribbons, verify their location on the sample rather than assuming they are present in the reference. Check the complete shipping pack for the intended distribution route; [ISTA describes different packaged-product procedure families](https://ista.org/test_procedures.php).

**Brief for MTT:** provide the largest wrapped item, item count and position map, target dimensions, color and logo artwork, quantity per design and shipping destination. Custom briefs start at 1,000 boxes per design; construction and fit are confirmed through sampling.

# Draft 2 — Half-Moon Gift Box: How to Check a Separate Lid and Curved Wall

**Candidate primary query:** custom half moon two piece rigid gift box. **Status:** draft, unpublished; editorial scoring and link verification pending.

A half-moon box is defined by two matching semicircular pieces: a base and a separate lift-off cover. Begin with the product envelope and the way the buyer will remove the lid. A visually attractive curve does not by itself establish the internal space, lid fit or whether a curved wall can be wrapped neatly in the selected paper.

## Specify the shape in the right view

Supply a front or plan view showing the straight edge and curve, then a side view showing box depth and cover height. Identify which dimensions describe the inside cavity and which describe the assembled outside. If an object or tray must fit, include its maximum envelope and the intended orientation. Do not substitute a circular diameter for a measured internal opening without checking board thickness, wrapping and the actual construction.

The buyer may want the finished box to stand on its flat edge for display. If so, state that requirement explicitly and evaluate stability with the actual contents, lid and display surface. A picture is not evidence that a loaded set will stand safely.

## Review curve, wrap and lid together

Ask the manufacturer how the selected wall and wrap will be formed along the curve, where the seam is expected and how the two ends meet the straight section. The cover and base must follow compatible curves without preventing normal removal. Thick or heavily textured wrap can change the fit and the appearance at the curved edge; inspect the proposed material in a production-representative sample before approving artwork.

Printed logos and foil require their own layout review. A mark placed across a curved transition may not read like a flat-screen mockup. Show the logo placement on an assembled sample, agree an orientation and inspect it in the viewing direction customers will use.

## Make the sample a decision tool

Test the empty lid first, then repeat with the actual insert and product. Record where the user grips the cover, whether the contents touch its interior and whether the base remains supported while the lid is removed. Review multiple samples when available and agree acceptance criteria with the supplier; no single lid clearance is appropriate for every board, wrap and size.

For delivery, specify whether covers and bases ship assembled, nested or separately, and how they are protected from abrasion. Use the loaded outer pack when assessing the appropriate transport-test plan. Quote the insert, finishes, tooling, samples and freight separately where applicable.

**Brief for MTT:** share a sketch, product measurements, planned standing or lying orientation, visual references, quantity and destination. Custom packaging starts at 1,000 pieces per design. The color images are concepts; final colors, branding and construction are developed to the customer's requirements.

# Draft 3 — Comparing Quotes for Shaped Rigid Boxes: Hinged Hexagon vs Half-Moon Lid

**Candidate primary query:** custom shaped rigid box quotation specifications. **Status:** draft, unpublished; editorial scoring and link verification pending.

When two suppliers quote a shaped gift box, the lower price may describe a different package. A hinged six-sided box with a radial insert and a separate-lid half-moon box are different assemblies. Compare each quote against a common brief and an agreed sample, then separate one-time costs from per-piece costs before deciding.

## Make the two structures explicit

For the hinged format, identify the cover-to-base connection, closing method if any, insert layout and the number of compartments. For the half-moon format, identify the separate lid, curved wall, straight edge and the intended presentation orientation. Give inside and outside dimensions separately and identify the exact object or protective wrapping included in the fit. Ask each supplier to identify assumptions on its quote.

A visual reference can communicate shape, color and mood. It cannot confirm board grade, hinge durability, compartment count, tested load or material certifications. Treat the image as a concept until a structural drawing and representative physical sample have been reviewed.

## Compare the same commercial scope

Ask for separate lines for the outer assembly, insert and each optional accessory, together with printing, logo finishes, tooling and sample charges. Specify the total quantity and the number of different artworks or sizes. A change in art versions or packing method can change setup and handling; do not assume a single unit price applies across every configuration.

Request the shipping basis, destination, packing arrangement and the dimensions and weight of the packed master carton when available. Mark preliminary freight figures as estimates and confirm trade terms and what is included before comparing them. Flat or curved shapes can pack differently even when the finished presentation areas appear similar.

## Use sampling to close the gaps

Agree which revision of the drawing and artwork each sample represents. Place the actual contents and accessories in the sample, check the opening and closing sequence, and record the dimensions and visible finish against the brief. Inspect the curved wrap, polygon corners or hinged area as appropriate to the structure. Check the fully packed set using a test plan suited to its actual distribution route; a supplier's sample approval is not a substitute for an agreed transport test.

Where a component is made from folding paperboard, the board and crease must be assessed together. [Holmen Iggesund's converting guidance](https://www.iggesund.com/insights/paperboard-know-how/paperboard-manual/paperboard-manual-publication/printing-and-converting-performance/die-cutting-and-creasing/) describes how crease design and material properties affect folding; it does not certify an individual finished rigid box.

**Brief for MTT:** send the reference shape, product list and dimensions, target quantity per design, logo file, proposed material or finish, optional accessories and destination. MTT can review custom briefs from 1,000 pieces per design; all product fit and material choices remain subject to sample approval.
